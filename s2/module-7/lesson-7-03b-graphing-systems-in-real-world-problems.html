📚 VOCABULARY

Coordinate plane: A flat surface made up of two number lines, the x-axis (horizontal) and the y-axis (vertical), that intersect at a point called the origin. It is used to graph points, lines, and shapes.

Infinitely many solutions: When the graphs of a system of equations are the same line, meaning every point on the line is a solution to the system.

Intersecting lines: Lines that cross each other at exactly one point. This point is the solution to a system of equations.

Linear equation: An equation that forms a straight line when graphed. It can be written in the form \(y=mx+b\), where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the y-intercept.

No solution: When the graphs of a system of equations are parallel and never intersect, meaning there is no set of x and y values that satisfy both equations.

Parallel lines: Lines in the same plane that never meet because they have the same slope but different y-intercepts.

Slope: A number that describes the steepness of a line. It is calculated as the rise (change in y) divided by the run (change in x).

Solution to a system of equations: The point or points where the graphs of the equations intersect. This represents the values of x and y that satisfy all equations in the system.

System of linear equations: Two or more linear equations that are analyzed together to find a common solution.

Y-intercept: The point where a line crosses the y-axis. It is the value of \(y\) when \(x=0\).

What Does the Solution to a System Mean?

A solution to a system of equations is the point or points that make all equations in the system true.

Graphically, it is the point where the lines represented by the equations intersect. Depending on the relationship between the lines, there are three possibilities:

  1. One Solution: The lines intersect at exactly one point.
  2. No Solution: The lines are parallel and never intersect.
  3. Infinitely Many Solutions: The lines overlap completely (coincide).



Steps to Solve a System by Graphing

  1. Identify the Slope and y-intercept of each equation: Identify the slope (m) and y-intercept (b) for each equation in slope-intercept form \(y=mx+b\).
  2. Graph the first line:
    • Start at the y-intercept (b).
    • Use the slope (m) to find additional points.
      • For example, if the slope is 2, move up 2 units and right 1 unit.
  3. Graph the second line using the same steps.
  4. Identify the solution:
    • If the lines intersect at one point, the coordinates of that point are the solution.
    • If the lines are parallel, there is no solution.
    • If the lines coincide, there are infinitely many solutions.
  5. Write the solution as an ordered pair \((x,y)\).


What is a System of Equations in the Real World?

A system of equations can be used to model situations where two different conditions or constraints need to be satisfied at the same time. Each equation represents a relationship between two variables, such as distance and time, price and quantity, or number of items produced and sold. Solving the system means finding the values that satisfy both equations simultaneously.

Graphically, the solution is the point where the two lines intersect. This point represents the specific values of the variables that make both equations true.


Example 1

Problem Context:
A student is raising money by selling cookies and brownies. Each cookie costs $1, and each brownie costs $2. The student sold a total of 20 items, and the total amount of money raised was $30.

Each cookie is \(x\) and each brownie is \(y\).

System of Equations:

Solution:

  • We begin with graphing each equation and mark the point where the two lines intersect.
  • We can identify the point of intersection as \((10,10)\).
  • Now, we will interpret this in terms of the context of the problem. What does this solution mean?
  • If cookies are \(x\) and brownies are \(y\), then this means the student sold 10 cookies and 10 brownies

Interpretation: Selling 10 cookies and 10 brownies satisfies both the total number of items sold and the total money raised.



Example 2

Problem Context:
Two friends, Alice and Bob, are saving money.

  • Alice starts with $20 and saves $5 each week.
  • Bob starts with $50 but only saves $2 per week.

After how many weeks will they have the same amount of money?

Step 1: Write the Equations:

  • Let \(x\) represent the number of weeks, and \(y\) represent the total amount saved.
  • Alice's equation would be \(y=5x+20\) \(\implies\) starting with $20 and saving $5 per week.
  • Bob's equation would be \(y=2x+50\) \(\implies\) starting with $50 and saving $2 per week.

Step 2: Graph the Equations:

  • Start at (0,20), and use the slope 5 to go up 5 units and right 1 unit.
  • Start at (0,50), and use the slope 2 to go up 2 units and right 1 unit.

Step 3: Find the Solution: The lines intersect at \((10,70)\).

Solution: After 10 weeks, both Alice and Bob will have saved $70.



Conclusion

Solving real-world problems with systems of equations allows us to model and analyze situations where two conditions or relationships must be satisfied. By writing equations, graphing them, and finding their intersection, we can identify the solution and interpret its meaning in context. Practice using these steps with different scenarios to strengthen your understanding and skills!
