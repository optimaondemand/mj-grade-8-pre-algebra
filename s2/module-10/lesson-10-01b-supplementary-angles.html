📚 VOCABULARY

Acute Angle: An acute angle is any angle that is less than 90 degrees. It looks sharp and narrow, kind of like the tip of a pencil or a pizza slice that's really thin.

Adjacent Angles: Adjacent angles are two angles that sit next to each other and share a common side and a common vertex (corner point), but they don't overlap. Think of them as neighbors who share a fence.

Algebraic Expression: An algebraic expression is a math phrase that can include numbers, variables (like x or y), and operation signs (like +, -, *, /). It's like a code that tells you how to combine different quantities.

Angle Measure: The angle measure tells you the size of an angle in degrees. It's like measuring how wide an angle opens, using a protractor as your tool.

Complementary Angles: Two angles are complementary if they add up to 90 degrees. It's as if they complement each other to form a right angle. If you know one angle, you can find its complement by subtracting from 90 degrees.

Linear Pair: A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles that form a straight line. Together, these angles add up to 180 degrees, just like a flat, straight road.

Obtuse Angle: An obtuse angle is any angle that is more than 90 degrees but less than 180 degrees. It's wider than an acute angle, kind of like the open mouth of an alligator.

Right Angle: A right angle is an angle that measures exactly 90 degrees. It's the corner of a square or rectangle, looking perfectly square and even marked with a special little square symbol.

Straight Angle: A straight angle is an angle that measures exactly 180 degrees. It looks like a straight line. If you open a book and lay it flat, the spine and the open edge form a straight angle.

Supplementary Angles: Two angles are supplementary if they add up to 180 degrees. You can think of them as two pieces that together form a straight line. If you know one angle, you can find its supplement by subtracting it from 180 degrees.

Vertical Angles: When two lines intersect, they form two pairs of opposite (or vertical) angles. These angles are always equal in measure. Imagine two crossing lines creating an "X"; the angles that are directly across from each other are the vertical angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore the concept of supplementary angles, a key idea in geometry that helps us understand how angles relate to each other, to straight angles, and to linear pairs. Supplementary angles are two angles whose measures add up to 180 degrees, the same as a straight line. This relationship is vital for solving various geometric problems and understanding the world around us, where angles are everywhere—from the edges of a book to the hands of a clock. By the end of this lesson, you'll know how to identify supplementary angles and calculate their measures, whether given in degrees or as algebraic expressions.


What Are Supplementary Angles?

Supplementary angles are a pair of angles that together form a straight angle, which measures 180 degrees. These angles can be adjacent, forming a linear pair (where they share a common side and a common vertex and line up to create a straight line), or they can be separate. The key point is their measures add up to 180 degrees, no more, no less.


Example 1

In the image, \(\angle BAD\) and \(\angle DAC\) are supplementary angles. The measure of \(\angle BAD\) is given. Find the measure of \(\angle DAC\).

Solution:

  • Since the angles are supplementary, then the sum of the angles makes \(180^\circ \).
  • We are given the measure of one angle. To find the measure of the other angle, we subtract the given measure from 180.
  • \(180^\circ -35.8^\circ =144.2^\circ \)
  • We find that \(m\angle DAC=144.2^\circ \)


Example 2

Two angles, \(\angle DGH\) and \(\angle LJM\), are supplementary. Given \(m\angle DGH=74^\circ \), find the \(m\angle LJM\).

Solution:

  • The notation of the little m in front of the angle symbol means measure. We need to find the measure of angle LJM.
  • Since the angles are supplementary, we can subtract the given angle measure from \(180^\circ \).
  • \(180^\circ -74^\circ =106^\circ \)
  • We find that \(m\angle LJM=106^\circ \).

**Note: Notice that when two angles are supplementary, one angle is obtuse, and one angle is acute. The only other pair of supplementary angles is if they are both right angles since \(90+90=180\).


Example 3

In the image, the measures of the angles are given as algebraic expressions. Find the exact measures of both angles, \(\angle BAD\) and \(\angle DAC\).

Solution:

  • To find the measures of the angles, we need to first solve for x. 
  • To solve for x, we will set up an equation using the fact the angles are supplementary. We add the two measures together and set the sum equal to 180.
  • \(3x+20+2x=180\)
  • Now, we can combine like terms and solve for x.
  • \(5x+20=180\)
    \(5x+20-20=180-20\)
    \(\frac{5x}{5}=\frac{160}{5}\)
    \(x=32\)
  • Now, that we know the value of x, we substitute it back into each expression to find the measures of the angles.
  • \(m\angle BAD=(2\cdot 32)^\circ =64^\circ \)
  • \(m\angle DAC=(3\cdot 32+20)^\circ =116^\circ \)
  • We find that \(m\angle BAD=64^\circ \) and \(m\angle DAC=116^\circ \).


Conclusion

Supplementary angles form the backbone of many geometric principles and real-life applications. Understanding how to identify and calculate these angles, whether they're forming a linear pair or given as algebraic expressions, enhances our ability to navigate the world of geometry with confidence. Today, we've seen how supplementary angles relate to straight angles and how to work with them in practical examples. Remember, the world of geometry is full of patterns and relationships waiting to be discovered, and supplementary angles are just one of the many fascinating aspects of this mathematical landscape.
