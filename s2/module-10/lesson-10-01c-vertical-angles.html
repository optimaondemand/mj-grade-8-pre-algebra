📚 VOCABULARY

Acute Angle: An acute angle is any angle that is less than 90 degrees. It looks sharp and narrow, kind of like the tip of a pencil or a pizza slice that's really thin.

Adjacent Angles: Adjacent angles are two angles that sit next to each other and share a common side and a common vertex (corner point), but they don't overlap. Think of them as neighbors who share a fence.

Algebraic Expression: An algebraic expression is a math phrase that can include numbers, variables (like x or y), and operation signs (like +, -, *, /). It's like a code that tells you how to combine different quantities.

Angle Measure: The angle measure tells you the size of an angle in degrees. It's like measuring how wide an angle opens, using a protractor as your tool.

Complementary Angles: Two angles are complementary if they add up to 90 degrees. It's as if they complement each other to form a right angle. If you know one angle, you can find its complement by subtracting from 90 degrees.

Linear Pair: A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles that form a straight line. Together, these angles add up to 180 degrees, just like a flat, straight road.

Obtuse Angle: An obtuse angle is any angle that is more than 90 degrees but less than 180 degrees. It's wider than an acute angle, kind of like the open mouth of an alligator.

Right Angle: A right angle is an angle that measures exactly 90 degrees. It's the corner of a square or rectangle, looking perfectly square and even marked with a special little square symbol.

Straight Angle: A straight angle is an angle that measures exactly 180 degrees. It looks like a straight line. If you open a book and lay it flat, the spine and the open edge form a straight angle.

Supplementary Angles: Two angles are supplementary if they add up to 180 degrees. You can think of them as two pieces that together form a straight line. If you know one angle, you can find its supplement by subtracting it from 180 degrees.

Vertical Angles: When two lines intersect, they form two pairs of opposite (or vertical) angles. These angles are always equal in measure. Imagine two crossing lines creating an "X"; the angles that are directly across from each other are the vertical angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we're going to unlock the mysteries surrounding vertical angles, a fundamental concept in geometry that appears when two lines intersect. Picture the letter "X"; the angles opposite each other at the intersection point are what we call vertical angles. These angles are special because they are always equal in measure. Vertical angles are formed because the lines crossing each other share a common vertex (the point where they meet), creating two pairs of opposite angles. Understanding vertical angles is crucial, not just for solving geometric problems but also for recognizing patterns and relationships in the world around us.


How Vertical Angles Are Formed

When two straight lines intersect, they form two pairs of vertical (or opposite) angles. These angles are called "vertical" because they share the same vertex. It’s like two people standing back-to-back; they might not be facing the same direction, but they’re connected at a single point.

No matter how the lines cross, the vertical angles are always equal to each other.


Example 1

In the image, \(\overline{DE}\) intersects \(\overline{BC}\) at point A. Angle BAE and angle DAC are vertical angles.

Find \(m\angle DAC\).

Solution:

  • Since \(\angle DAC\) is vertical to \(\angle BAE\), their angle measures are equal.
  • Therefore, \(m\angle DAC=147.74^\circ \).


Example 2

Let's consider two intersecting lines that form vertical angles, \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\).

If \(m\angle A=45^\circ \), then what is \(m\angle B\)?

Solution:

  • Since the angles are vertical, their measures are equal.
  • Therefore, \(m\angle B=45^\circ \).


Example 3

Imagine two intersecting lines that create a pair of vertical angles, \(\angle X\) and \(\angle Y\). The \(m\angle X=(2x+30)^\circ \) and \(m\angle Y=(3x-15)^\circ \).

Find the exact measures of both angles.

Solution:

  • Since the angles are vertical, we can set the algebraic expressions representing their measures equal to each other. This will create a linear equation we can use to solve for x.
  • \(2x+30=3x-15\)
    \(2x-2x+30=3x-2x-15\)
    \(30=x-15\)
    \(30+15=x-15+15\)
    \(45=x\)
  • We find that \(x=45\). Now that we know the value of x, we can substitute the value into one of the expressions to find its exact measure. Since they are vertical, once we find one measure, the other one will be the exact same measure.
  • \(3(45)-15=135-15=120\)
  • We know that \(m\angle X=120^\circ \) and \(m\angle Y=120^\circ \).


Conclusion

Today, we delved into the concept of vertical angles, discovering how they are always equal in measure due to their formation from intersecting lines. We explored examples that show how to calculate the measure of vertical angles, whether given directly or through algebraic expressions. Understanding vertical angles enhances your geometry skills, allowing you to see the symmetry and balance in the patterns that emerge when lines intersect. Keep practicing with different sets of vertical angles, and soon, identifying and calculating their measures will become second nature to you.
