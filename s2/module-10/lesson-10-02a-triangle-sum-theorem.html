📚 VOCABULARY

Acute Angle: An acute angle is any angle that is less than 90 degrees. It's like a sharp corner that's less open than the corner of a square.

Algebraic Expression: An algebraic expression is a math phrase that includes numbers, variables (like letters that stand for numbers), and operation signs (like +, -, *, /). It's a way to describe mathematical ideas without using a full equation.

Angle: An angle is formed when two lines meet at a point. Think of it as the opening or space between two lines or rays that start from the same point. Angles are measured in degrees.

Exterior Angle: An exterior angle of a triangle is an angle that forms outside the triangle when you extend one of its sides. It's like if you opened a door slightly; the space between the door and the door frame on the outside is similar to an exterior angle.

Exterior Angle Theorem: This theorem says that the measure of an exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the measures of the two non-adjacent interior angles. It's like saying the outside angle is a combination of the two farthest inside angles you can find.

Interior Angle: An interior angle is an angle that is inside a shape. For a triangle, it's any of the three angles formed by the sides of the triangle.

Non-Adjacent Interior Angles: These are interior angles in a triangle that are not next to each other. If you're looking at one angle of a triangle, the non-adjacent interior angles are the other two angles.

Supplementary Angles: Two angles are supplementary if their measures add up to 180 degrees, like a straight line. If you have one angle that is 120 degrees, its supplementary angle would be 60 degrees because together they make a straight line.

Triangle: A triangle is a shape with three sides and three angles. Think of it like a piece of pie or a road sign; it always has three corners and three straight sides.

Triangle Sum Theorem: This theorem states that the sum of the interior angles of a triangle is always 180 degrees. No matter what kind of triangle you have, if you add up all of its inside angles, the total will be 180 degrees.

What is the Triangle Sum Theorem?

The Triangle Sum Theorem states that in any triangle, the total degrees of all three interior angles add up to exactly 180 degrees.
