📚 VOCABULARY

Acute Angle: An acute angle is any angle that is less than 90 degrees. It looks sharp and narrow, kind of like the tip of a pencil or a pizza slice that's really thin.

Adjacent Angles: Adjacent angles are two angles that sit next to each other and share a common side and a common vertex (corner point), but they don't overlap. Think of them as neighbors who share a fence.

Algebraic Expression: An algebraic expression is a math phrase that can include numbers, variables (like x or y), and operation signs (like +, -, *, /). It's like a code that tells you how to combine different quantities.

Angle Measure: The angle measure tells you the size of an angle in degrees. It's like measuring how wide an angle opens, using a protractor as your tool.

Complementary Angles: Two angles are complementary if they add up to 90 degrees. It's as if they complement each other to form a right angle. If you know one angle, you can find its complement by subtracting from 90 degrees.

Linear Pair: A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles that form a straight line. Together, these angles add up to 180 degrees, just like a flat, straight road.

Obtuse Angle: An obtuse angle is any angle that is more than 90 degrees but less than 180 degrees. It's wider than an acute angle, kind of like the open mouth of an alligator.

Right Angle: A right angle is an angle that measures exactly 90 degrees. It's the corner of a square or rectangle, looking perfectly square and even marked with a special little square symbol.

Straight Angle: A straight angle is an angle that measures exactly 180 degrees. It looks like a straight line. If you open a book and lay it flat, the spine and the open edge form a straight angle.

Supplementary Angles: Two angles are supplementary if they add up to 180 degrees. You can think of them as two pieces that together form a straight line. If you know one angle, you can find its supplement by subtracting from 180 degrees.

Vertical Angles: When two lines intersect, they form two pairs of opposite (or vertical) angles. These angles are always equal in measure. Imagine two crossing lines creating an "X"; the angles that are directly across from each other are the vertical angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In our journey through the fascinating world of geometry, we encounter various types of angles that help us understand the shapes and structures around us. In today's lesson, we're going to focus on adjacent angles and discover how their measures relate to each other. Adjacent angles are like the building blocks of geometry, helping us piece together more complex geometric figures.


What Are Adjacent Angles?

Adjacent angles are two angles that have a common side and a common vertex but do not overlap. Imagine two slices of pizza touching at a corner; the crust side they share is like the common side of adjacent angles, and the pointy tip where they meet is like the common vertex. These angles sit next to each other, forming a unique relationship that's key to understanding many geometric concepts.

How the Measures of Adjacent Angles Are Related

The relationship between the measures of adjacent angles depends on what they form together. Often, adjacent angles combine to create a larger angle or a specific shape.


Adjacent angles can be formed by two intersecting lines like this example.

Since the lines intersect each other, the adjacent angles form a linear pair. This means the two angles are supplementary and together equal \(180^\circ \).

Adjacent angles share a common side. In this example, the angles \(\angle DAC\) and \(\angle CAB\) share side AC. The sum of their measures will equal \(\angle DAB\).

Therefore, \(m\angle DAC+ m\angle CAB=m\angle DAB\). Since \(m\angle DAB=180^\circ \), then \(m\angle DAC+m\angle CAB=180^\circ \). We can find this to be true since \(129+51=180\).


We can use the relationship of adjacent angles to find a missing angle if we know the measure of the whole angle formed by the adjacent pair. Let's look at another example of adjacent angles.


In this image, the angles \(\angle DAC\) and \(\angle CAB\) are adjacent angles since they share the side ray AC. The sum of their measures will equal \(m\angle DAB\). We can find \(m\angle DAB\) by finding the sum \(93.2^\circ +36.8^\circ \). We find that \(m\angle DAB=93.2+36.8=130^\circ \).


Example 1

In the image \(\overline{BD}\) and \(\overline{CE}\) intersect at point A. The measure of \(\angle CAD\) is shown.

Find the measure of \(\angle DAE\).

Solution:

  • Since the lines intersect, they form linear pairs. Angle CAD and angle DAE are adjacent and form a linear pair. This makes them supplementary.
  • The sum of their angle measures is \(180^\circ \). So, to find the measure of \(\angle DAE\), subtract the measure of \(\angle CAD\) from \(180^\circ \).
  • \(m\angle DAE=180^\circ -112^\circ =68^\circ \)

We find that \(m\angle DAE=68^\circ \).


Example 2

In the image, \(\angle BAC\) and \(\angle CAD\) share side AC. The measure of \(\angle CAD\) is shown and \(m\angle BAD=144.9^\circ \).

Find the measure of \(\angle BAC\).

Solution:

  • To find the measure of the angle, the sum of \(\angle BAC\) and \(\angle CAD\) will equal the measure of \(\angle BAD\).
  • So, \(m\angle BAC=144.9^\circ -76.9^\circ =68^\circ \).


Conclusion

Adjacent angles are a fundamental concept in geometry, revealing the relationships between angles that share a side and a vertex. By exploring how the measures of adjacent angles relate, especially when they form linear pairs or contribute to the structure of geometric shapes, we gain deeper insights into the mathematical world. Remember, geometry is all about connections and relationships, and adjacent angles beautifully illustrate how individual parts come together to form a bigger picture. Keep practicing with adjacent angles, and soon, you'll see how they help unlock the secrets of more complex geometric figures.
