📚 VOCABULARY

Algebraic Expression: An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase that includes numbers, variables (like letters that represent unknown values), and operation signs (such as plus, minus, times, and divided by). It does not have an equal sign, so it's not a complete equation but rather a part of one.

Decomposition: Decomposition in math usually refers to breaking down a complex shape or number into simpler parts. For example, decomposing a polygon might involve dividing it into triangles, which can make it easier to calculate areas or other properties.

Exterior Angle: An exterior angle is formed by one side of a shape (like a triangle or polygon) and the extension of an adjacent side. In simpler terms, if you imagine stretching out one side of a triangle, the angle formed outside the triangle is the exterior angle.

Interior Angle: An interior angle is an angle that is inside a shape, such as a triangle or polygon. For instance, any of the three angles inside a triangle that add up to the triangle’s corners are interior angles.

Polygon: A polygon is a flat shape that has straight sides. Examples include triangles (3 sides), rectangles (4 sides), and pentagons (5 sides). Polygons can have any number of sides that are closed in a loop.

Regular Polygon: A regular polygon is a special type of polygon where all the sides are of equal length and all the interior angles are equal. Examples are a square (a regular quadrilateral) or an equilateral triangle (a regular triangle).

Sum of Interior Angles: This refers to the total measure of all the interior angles in a polygon. There's a neat formula to remember: for any polygon with n sides, the sum of the interior angles is degrees. This helps you find how much all the inside corners add up to.

Triangle: A triangle is a three-sided polygon and is the simplest form of a polygon. It has three edges and three vertices (corners), and it's a fundamental shape in geometry.

Triangle Sum Theorem: This theorem states that the sum of all the interior angles in a triangle is always 180 degrees. No matter what type of triangle it is, the angles inside it will always add up to 180 degrees.

Why the Formula Works:

  • Decomposition into Triangles: Any polygon can be divided (or decomposed) into triangles by drawing diagonals from one vertex to all the other vertices that are not directly next to it.
  • Triangles' Sum: Since each triangle's interior angles sum to 180 degrees, and a polygon can be decomposed into n-2 triangles (where n is the number of sides), multiplying n-2 by 180 degrees gives us the sum of all interior angles of the polygon.
