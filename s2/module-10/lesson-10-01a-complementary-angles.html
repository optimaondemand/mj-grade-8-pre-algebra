📚 VOCABULARY

Acute Angle: An acute angle is any angle that is less than 90 degrees. It looks sharp and narrow, kind of like the tip of a pencil or a pizza slice that's really thin.

Adjacent Angles: Adjacent angles are two angles that sit next to each other and share a common side and a common vertex (corner point), but they don't overlap. Think of them as neighbors who share a fence.

Algebraic Expression: An algebraic expression is a math phrase that can include numbers, variables (like x or y), and operation signs (like +, -, *, /). It's like a code that tells you how to combine different quantities.

Angle Measure: The angle measure tells you the size of an angle in degrees. It's like measuring how wide an angle opens, using a protractor as your tool.

Complementary Angles: Two angles are complementary if they add up to 90 degrees. It's as if they complement each other to form a right angle. If you know one angle, you can find its complement by subtracting from 90 degrees.

Linear Pair: A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles that form a straight line. Together, these angles add up to 180 degrees, just like a flat, straight road.

Obtuse Angle: An obtuse angle is any angle that is more than 90 degrees but less than 180 degrees. It's wider than an acute angle, kind of like the open mouth of an alligator.

Right Angle: A right angle is an angle that measures exactly 90 degrees. It's the corner of a square or rectangle, looking perfectly square and even marked with a special little square symbol.

Straight Angle: A straight angle is an angle that measures exactly 180 degrees. It looks like a straight line. If you open a book and lay it flat, the spine and the open edge form a straight angle.

Supplementary Angles: Two angles are supplementary if they add up to 180 degrees. You can think of them as two pieces that together form a straight line. If you know one angle, you can find its supplement by subtracting from 180 degrees.

Vertical Angles: When two lines intersect, they form two pairs of opposite (or vertical) angles. These angles are always equal in measure. Imagine two crossing lines creating an "X"; the angles that are directly across from each other are the vertical angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore the concept of complementary angles, a fundamental aspect of geometry that reveals how angles can work together in pairs to create a right angle. Complementary angles are two angles whose measures add up to 90 degrees. This concept not only helps us understand the relationships between different angles but also enables us to solve various geometric problems. By learning how to identify and calculate complementary angles, you'll gain insights into how geometry functions both in theory and in the real world around us.


What Are Complementary Angles?

Complementary angles are two angles whose measures add up to 90 degrees. Think of them as two friends who, together, have exactly 90 dollars to spend. One might have 30 dollars, and the other has 60 dollars; combined, they have 90 dollars. In the world of angles, if one angle measures 30 degrees, its complementary angle measures 60 degrees because together, they must reach 90 degrees.

How Are Complementary Angles Related?

The relationship between complementary angles is rooted in their definition. No matter how big or small one of the angles is, its complement will always make up the difference to reach 90 degrees. This relationship is particularly useful in solving problems where you might know the measure of one angle and need to find the measure of its complement.

\(\angle EDF\) and \(\angle BAC\) are complementary angles because \(48^\circ +42^\circ =90^\circ \).


Example 1

In the image, \(\angle BAC\) is a right angle. The measure of \(\angle BAD\) is shown. Find the measure of \(\angle DAC\).

Solution:

  • Since \(\angle BAC\) is a right angle, it equals 90 degrees. With ray AD cutting the angle into two angles, then \(\angle BAD\) are complementary angles.
  • Since we know the measure of \(\angle BAD\), we can find the measure of \(\angle DAC\) by subtracting the given measure from 90.
  • \(m\angle DAC=90-37.61=52.39^\circ \)


Example 2

The angles \(\angle DEG\) and \(\angle HIK\) are complementary angles. The \(m\angle HIK=34^\circ\), find the measure of \(\angle DEG\).

Solution:

  • Since the angles are complementary, together they make 90.
  • We know the measure of one angle. To find the measure of the other angle, we subtract from 90.
  • \(m\angle DEG=90-34=56^\circ \)


Example 3

In the image, \(m\angle BAD=(2x+10)^\circ \) and \(m\angle DAC=(7x+17)^\circ \). The angles are complementary angles. Find the measure of \(\angle DAC\).

Solution:

  • To find the measure of \(\angle DAC\), we know that the two angles are complementary. Therefore, the sums of their measures equal 90.
  • The measures are expressed as algebraic expressions. So, we can create an equation and solve for x by combining like terms.
  • \((7x+17)+(2x+10)=90\)
    \(9x+27=90\)
    \(9x+27-27=90-27\)
    \(9x=63\)
    \(x=7\)
  • Now, that we know what x equals, we substitute x back into the expression for \(\angle DAC\) to find its measure.
  • \(m\angle DAC=7(7)+17=49+17=66^\circ \)

\(m\angle DAC=66^\circ\)


Conclusion

Today, we've explored complementary angles, see how they add up to create a right angle, and worked through examples ranging from visual scenarios to algebraic expressions. Understanding complementary angles enriches our knowledge of geometry and enhances our ability to solve problems involving angles. As you continue your studies in geometry, remember that the relationships between angles, such as complementary pairs, are key to unlocking the secrets of the shapes and patterns we encounter every day. Keep practicing with different examples, and soon, identifying and calculating complementary angles will become second nature.
