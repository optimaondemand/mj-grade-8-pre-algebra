In a previous lesson, we learned about angles, focusing on how they form and how they are categorized. Today, we will delve deeper into the specific types of angles found in triangles: interior angles and exterior angles. These concepts are crucial for advancing your understanding of geometry and solving more complex problems that involve triangles.

What is an Interior Angle?

Interior angles are those angles that are located within the boundary of a triangle. They are the corners of the triangle, where two sides meet.

  • Characteristics of Interior Angles:
    • They are formed by the intersection of two sides of a triangle.
    • The sum of all interior angles in any triangle is always 180 degrees, according to the Triangle Sum Theorem.
    • Each triangle has three interior angles.

What is an Exterior Angle?

Exterior angles are formed by one side of the triangle and the extension of an adjacent side. These angles are outside the triangle and are key when considering various properties of triangles, such as in proofs and solving for unknown angles.

  • Characteristics of Exterior Angles:
    • An exterior angle is formed when a side of a triangle is extended.
    • The measure of an exterior angle is equal to the sum of the measures of the two non-adjacent interior angles, according to the Exterior Angle Theorem.
    • There can be six exterior angles in any triangle, as each vertex allows for two different exterior angles, depending on which side is extended.
