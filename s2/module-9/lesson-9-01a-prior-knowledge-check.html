In a previous grade, you learned about finding the sample space of a simple experiment. As we get ready to explore sample spaces of represted experiments, it is important to review what a sample space is and how to determine the sample space of a simple experiment.

What is a Sample Space?

A sample space is the set of all possible outcomes of a probability experiment. It includes every result that could happen when the event takes place. Think of it as a list or collection of everything that could occur.


How to Determine Sample Space

To determine the sample space of a probability event, follow these steps:

  1. Identify the Experiment: Understand what the experiment or activity is. For example, rolling a die, flipping a coin, or drawing a card from a deck.
  2. List Possible Outcomes: Write down every outcome that can result from the experiment, without leaving any out.
  3. Organize the Outcomes: Arrange these outcomes in a structured way, often as a list or a set, to clearly see the sample space.

When determining the sample space, each unique outcome is listed only once, regardless of how many times it appears or is represented in the experiment. The sample space focuses on the types of possible outcomes, not the probability of those outcomes.

Conclusion

Understanding the sample space is like having a map before starting a journey. It shows you all the paths (outcomes) you might take. By learning how to determine the sample space of different probability events, you're building a strong foundation in probability. This knowledge helps you better understand the chances of various outcomes and makes solving probability problems easier and more accurate. Remember, the first step in any probability question is to clearly outline the sample space, because knowing all possible outcomes is key to finding the answers you seek.
