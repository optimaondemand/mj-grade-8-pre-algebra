📚 VOCABULARY

Association: In the context of scatter plots, association refers to the relationship or pattern you can observe between two sets of data. If one set of data changes, the association describes how the other set might change in response. This can be positive (both increase), negative (one decreases as the other increases), or none (no observable pattern).

Bivariate Data: Bivariate data involves two variables or sets of data. When we graph bivariate data, each data point represents two connected values, allowing us to see relationships or patterns between the two variables.

Equation of Line of Fit: This is a mathematical formula that describes the best line that represents the trend shown by the data points in a scatter plot. It's usually in the form of \(y=mx+b\) where is the slope of the line and  is the y-intercept. This equation helps us make predictions about one variable based on the other.

Line of Fit: Also known as a trend line, it's a straight line drawn through the center of a group of data points plotted on a scatter plot. This line summarizes the relationship between the two variables, showing the general direction the data is moving (upwards, downwards, or level).

Linear Model: A linear model is a type of mathematical model that assumes a linear relationship between two variables. It's used to describe how one variable changes with respect to another, using a line of best fit or equation of line of fit.

Outlier: An outlier is a data point that is significantly different from the other data points in a data set. In a scatter plot, an outlier is a point that lies far away from the other points, and it can affect the slope and position of the line of fit.

Prediction: A prediction is a guess about a value that is not in the data set but could be expected based on the pattern shown by the data. Using the equation of the line of fit, we can make predictions about future or unknown values.

Scatter Plot: A scatter plot is a type of graph used to represent bivariate data. It displays individual data points plotted on a two-dimensional graph, with one variable along the x-axis and the other along the y-axis. Scatter plots are used to look for trends, patterns, and associations between the variables.

Strength: In the context of scatter plots, strength refers to how closely the data points follow a clear form or line. A strong association means the points closely follow a trend (linear or otherwise), making the relationship between variables clear. Weak strength means the points are more scattered and do not follow a clear trend, indicating a weaker relationship.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we'll explore scatter plots, a type of graph that shows the relationship between two variables in a data set. By examining scatter plots, we can identify patterns and associations between variables, helping us make predictions and understand the world around us.


What is a Scatter Plot?

A scatter plot is a graph that uses dots to represent the values of two different variables. One variable is plotted along the x-axis (horizontal), and the other is plotted along the y-axis (vertical). The position of each dot on the graph shows the values of both variables for that data point.


Identifying Patterns in Scatter Plots

When we look at a scatter plot, we're interested in seeing if there's a pattern that shows a relationship between the two variables. This can be:

  • Positive association: As one variable increases, the other variable also increases.
  • Negative association: As one variable increases, the other variable decreases.
  • No association: There is no clear pattern between the variables.


Example 1

Solution:

To create a scatter plot, we will plot the points for each pair of data in the given table. The temperature will be the independent variable on the x-axis and the dependent variable will be the number of ice creams sold on the y-axis. So the first data point would be (60, 50).

We can look at the range of temperatures to determine the range of the x-axis. We will have the x-axis range from 58 to 87 to go a little before and a little beyond the average temperatures in the table. We will have the y-axis range from 48 to 102. Now, we can create a coordinate plane and graph our points. You can do this using desmos.com.

Now we analyze the patterns we see and describe what we find.

When we plot this data, we see a scatter plot where points trend upwards from left to right. This indicates a positive association between temperature and ice cream sales: as the temperature increases, so does the number of ice creams sold.

Conclusion

Scatter plots are powerful tools for visualizing and understanding the relationships between variables. By examining the pattern of dots, you can determine whether there is a positive, negative, or no association between the variables. This skill is valuable in many real-world situations, from business to science, helping us make informed decisions and understandings.
