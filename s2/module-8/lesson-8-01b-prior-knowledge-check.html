📖 PRIOR KNOWLEDGE

In previous lessons, we learned about the slope of a linear equation and what it means. As we get ready to explore informally drawing lines of fit and describing the association of the line, it is important to review the different types of slope and what this means in terms of what the function is doing. Understanding slope is crucial because it tells us how steep a line is and the direction it moves across a graph. We'll define positive slope and negative slope look at what their graphs resemble, and relate the slope type to whether the function is increasing, decreasing, or remaining constant.


What is Slope?

The slope of a line measures the rate at which the y-value of a point on the line changes with respect to the x-value of the point. In simpler terms, it tells us how steep the line is and in which direction it goes.

 

Positive Slope

  • Definition: A line has a positive slope when it moves upwards from left to right. This means that as the x-value increases, the y-value also increases.
  • Graph Appearance: The line slants diagonally upwards.
  • Function Behavior: The function is increasing. This indicates that as one variable goes up, the other variable also goes up.

The slope of this graph is positive which means the line is increasing as it moves from left to right.


Negative Slope

  • Definition: A line has a negative slope when it moves downwards from left to right. This means that as the x-value increases, the y-value decreases.
  • Graph Appearance: The line slants diagonally downwards.
  • Function Behavior: The function is decreasing. This indicates that as one variable goes up, the other variable goes down.

The slope of this graph is negative which means the line is decreasing as it moves from left to right.


Conclusion

Understanding the different types of slopes and their implications on the behavior of linear functions is fundamental in algebra. Positive slopes indicate increasing functions, negative slopes indicate decreasing functions, zero slopes indicate constant functions, and undefined slopes are associated with vertical lines. Recognizing these characteristics will help you graph linear equations accurately and understand their real-world applications.
