📚 VOCABULARY

Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem: The Converse of the Pythagorean Theorem states that if the square of the longest side of a triangle (often called the hypotenuse) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides, then the triangle is a right triangle. In other words, this rule helps us determine if a triangle with certain side lengths can be a right triangle.

Coordinate: A coordinate is a number used to identify the location of a point on a graph or grid. It is part of a set of numbers used in a coordinate system, like the x-coordinate and y-coordinate on a coordinate plane, which show positions along the horizontal and vertical axes, respectively.

Coordinate Plane: The coordinate plane is a two-dimensional surface on which each point is determined by a pair of numbers, called coordinates. These coordinates correspond to distances along two perpendicular lines or axes: the horizontal axis (x-axis) and the vertical axis (y-axis).

Distance: Distance is a measure of how far apart two points or objects are from each other. In mathematics, it's often calculated as the straight line between two points in a space, like on a coordinate plane.

Hypotenuse: The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle, lying opposite the right angle. It is the side used in the Pythagorean Theorem to find the relationship between the lengths of the sides of a right triangle.

Leg: In the context of right triangles, a leg refers to one of the two sides that form the right angle. There are always two legs in a right triangle, and they meet to form the right angle.

Pythagorean Theorem: The Pythagorean Theorem is a fundamental principle in geometry which states that in a right triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides (the legs). This theorem is essential for calculating distances and understanding right triangles.

Right Triangle: A right triangle is a type of triangle that has one angle measuring exactly 90 degrees, known as the right angle. The sides forming this right angle are called the legs, and the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to use one of the most fundamental and powerful tools in geometry: the Pythagorean Theorem. This theorem is not just a formula; it is a bridge that connects the three sides of a right triangle. Understanding how to apply the Pythagorean Theorem can help you solve numerous problems in mathematics, especially when one side of a right triangle is unknown. Let’s dive into how this theorem can be used to find the length of a missing side when the lengths of the other two sides are given.

Understanding the Pythagorean Theorem

The Pythagorean Theorem states that in a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the longest side of the triangle, opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides (known as the legs). The formula can be expressed as: \(c^2=a^2+b^2\). Here, \(c\) is the hypotenuse, and \(a\) and \(b\) are the legs of the triangle.


Applying the Pythagorean Theorem

To find a missing side of a right triangle using the Pythagorean Theorem, you need to know which side is missing:

  1. If the hypotenuse is missing: You simply add the squares of the two legs and then take the square root of the result.
  2. If one of the legs is missing: You subtract the square of the known leg from the square of the hypotenuse and then take the square root of the result.


Example 1

Finding the Hypotenuse

The image shows a right triangle where the two legs are labeled with a length. Find the length of the hypotenuse.

Solution:

  • Step 1: Square the lengths of the legs: \(6^2=36\) and \(8^2=64\).
  • Step 2: Add these squares: \(36+64=100\).
  • Step 3: Take the square root of the sum to find the hypotenuse: \(\sqrt{100}=10\).

Thus, the hypotenuse is 10 units.


Example 2

Finding a Missing Leg

The image shows a right triangle with two side lengths given.

Find the measure of the third side length. Express the length as an exact value and as an approximation rounded to the nearest thousandth.

Solution:

  • Step 1: Square the hypotenuse and the known leg: \(12^2=144\) and \(7^2=49\).
  • Step 2: Subtract the square of the known leg from the square of the hypotenuse: \(144-49=95\).
  • Step 3: Take the square root of the result to find the length of the missing leg: \(\sqrt{95}\approx 9.75\).

The exact value of the side length is \(\sqrt{95}\).

Using a calculator, we can approximate the length as \(9.75\).


Conclusion

Today's lesson has shown you how to effectively use the Pythagorean Theorem to solve for missing sides in right triangles. Whether you're dealing with the hypotenuse or one of the legs, this theorem provides a clear and systematic approach to solving these types of geometric problems. Mastering this technique will not only aid your understanding of geometry but will also enhance your problem-solving skills across various mathematical contexts.
