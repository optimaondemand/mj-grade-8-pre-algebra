📚 VOCABULARY

Feasible Set: A collection of all possible points or values that satisfy a given set of conditions or inequalities; in geometry, it refers to sets of side lengths that can form a triangle.

Inequality: A mathematical statement showing that two values are not equal and that one is either greater than or less than the other.

Side Lengths: The measurements of the edges of a polygon; for triangles, these are the three segments that connect the vertices.

Triangle: A polygon with three sides and three angles.

Triangle Inequality Theorem: A rule stating that the sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the length of the third side for a triangle to exist.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore the Triangle Inequality Theorem and how it helps us determine whether three given side lengths can form a triangle. You already know that a triangle is a polygon with three sides. But not every set of three side lengths will make a triangle. Using the Triangle Inequality Theorem, we can test sets of side lengths and decide if they are feasible, meaning they can actually form a triangle.


What is the Triangle Inequality Theorem?

The Triangle Inequality Theorem says that for any three side lengths, the sum of the lengths of any two sides must be greater than the length of the third side. This rule must be true for all three combinations of sides.

In mathematical terms, for side lengths \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\);

\(a+b>c\)

\(a+c>b\)

\(b+c>a\)

If all three inequalities are true, the side lengths form a triangle. If even one of the inequalities is false, a triangle cannot be formed.



Example 1

Given side lengths 5, 7, and 10, can these sides form a triangle?

Check:

\(5+7=12 \implies 12>10\checkmark \)

\(5+10=15 \implies 15>7\checkmark \)

\(7+10=17 \implies 17>5\checkmark \)

Since all inequalities are true, yes, these side lengths can form a triangle.



Example 2

Given side lengths 3, 4, and 8, can these sides form a triangle?

Check:

\(3+4=7 \implies 7<8 \)X

\(3+8=11 \implies 11>4\checkmark \)

\(4+8=12 \implies 12>3\checkmark \)

Because \(3+4\) is not greater than \(8\), these side lengths cannot form a triangle.



Conclusion

Today you learned that not all sets of side lengths can form a triangle. By using the Triangle Inequality Theorem, you can test three lengths to see if they satisfy the necessary conditions. Remember, the sum of any two sides must always be greater than the third side. This rule is important in many areas of geometry and helps you understand the basic properties of shapes. With practice, you’ll quickly be able to determine whether a triangle can be formed just by looking at the side lengths!
