📚 VOCABULARY

Feasible Set: A collection of all possible points or values that satisfy a given set of conditions or inequalities; in geometry, it refers to sets of side lengths that can form a triangle.

Inequality: A mathematical statement showing that two values are not equal and that one is either greater than or less than the other.

Side Lengths: The measurements of the edges of a polygon; for triangles, these are the three segments that connect the vertices.

Triangle: A polygon with three sides and three angles.

Triangle Inequality Theorem: A rule stating that the sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the length of the third side for a triangle to exist.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to use the Triangle Inequality Theorem to determine possible values for a third side of a triangle when you are given two side lengths. You already know that not all sets of three numbers can form a triangle. Now, we will use what you have learned to figure out what the third side could be. Sometimes you may have to choose the correct third side from a list of options, and sometimes you may need to describe all possible values for the third side.


Understanding the Triangle Inequality Theorem

The Triangle Inequality Theorem says that for any three side lengths, the sum of the lengths of any two sides must be greater than the length of the third side. This rule must be true for all three combinations of sides.

In mathematical terms, for side lengths \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\);

\(a+b>c\)

\(a+c>b\)

\(b+c>a\)

When you are given two sides and trying to find a possible third side, you are looking for a number that makes all three inequalities true.


Finding a Range for the Third Side

When you know two sides, you can find the range for the third side by following these steps:

  1. Add the two given sides — the third side must be less than this sum.

  2. Find the positive difference between the two given sides — the third side must be greater than this difference.

In other words \(Difference<Third\; Side<Sum\).



Example 1

Given the side lengths 4 and 11, what could the third side be?

Solution:

  • Sum of the two given sides \(4+11=15\)
  • Difference of two given sides \(11-4=7\)
  • Thus, the third side must be greater than 7 and less than 15.
  • A possible 3rd side would be 9.



Example 2

Given side lengths 7 and 22, which of the following could be the third side?

A. 7

B. 18

C. 11

D. 30

E. 25

Solution:

  • Now, we will check each option.
  • Must be greater than \(22-7=15\) and less than \(22+7=29\).
  • \(7<15\) X - too small
  • \(18>15\) and \(18<29\) \(\checkmark \)
  • \(11<15\) X - too small
  • \(30>29\) X - too big
  • \(25>15\) and \(25<29\) \(\checkmark \)

Possible 3rd sides are 18 and 25.



Conclusion

Today, you learned how to use the Triangle Inequality Theorem to figure out possible third sides for a triangle. By adding and subtracting the two given side lengths, you can create a range that the third side must fit into. Understanding this helps you recognize whether a set of numbers can form a triangle and helps you solve problems quickly. With practice, you will become confident in identifying possible third sides, whether you are asked to find a range or choose from a list of options!
