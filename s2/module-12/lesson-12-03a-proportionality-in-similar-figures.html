📚 VOCABULARY

Corresponding Angles: Angles in the same relative position in similar or congruent shapes.

Corresponding Sides: Sides that are in the same relative position in similar or congruent shapes.

Dilation: A transformation that changes the size of a figure but keeps its shape, often based on a scale factor.

Proportionality: A relationship where two quantities increase or decrease at the same rate.

Proportional Relationship: A relationship between two ratios or rates that are equal.

Similarity: A relationship between two shapes that have the same shape but may have different sizes, with corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides in proportion.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore the concept of similar triangles and how we can use their properties to solve problems. Two triangles are similar when they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. This means their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are in proportion. Understanding this relationship allows us to write proportionality statements and solve for unknown side lengths. This skill is especially useful when solving real-world and geometric problems involving scaled drawings or enlarged/reduced figures.


What Are Similar Triangles?

Two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional. This means that:

  • The angles in one triangle match the angles in the other.

  • The sides of one triangle are all multiplied (or divided) by the same number to get the sides of the other triangle. This number is called the scale factor.

To compare two triangles:

  1. Identify the corresponding parts — which sides and angles go together.

  2. Write a proportionality statement — match the ratios of the corresponding side lengths.

  3. Use the proportion to solve for any unknown value.

Triangle ABC is similar to Triangle FGH

\(\textcolor{red}{\overline{AB}}\) corresponds to \(\textcolor{red}{\overline{FG}}\)

\(\textcolor{green}{\overline{BC}}\) corresponds to \(\textcolor{green}{\overline{GH}}\)

\(\textcolor{blue}{\overline{AC}}\) corresponds to \(\textcolor{blue}{\overline{FH}}\)

When we compare corresponding sides, we can find the scale factor and proportional relationships.

\(\textcolor{red}{\frac{AB}{FG}}=\textcolor{green}{\frac{BC}{GH}}\) = \(\textcolor{red}{\frac{8}{20}}=\textcolor{green}{\frac{12}{30}}\) = \(\frac{2}{5}\)

You can set up a proportion with any set of corresponding sides.












Example 1

Given that \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) is similar to \(\bigtriangleup EFG\), where \(AC=6\), \(AB=8\), and \(EG=9\).

Find the length of \(\overline{EF}\).

Solution:

Step 1 - Identify corresponding sides

AB corresponds to EF

AC corresponds to EG

Step 2 - Set up Proportion using corresponding sides

\(\frac{AB}{EF}=\frac{AC}{EG} \implies \frac{8}{x}=\frac{6}{9}\)

Note that we used a variable x to represent the length of EF since that is what we are looking for.

Step 3 - Solve the proportion using cross-multiplication

\(\frac{8}{x}=\frac{6}{9} \implies 6\cdot x=9\cdot 8 \implies 6x=72 \implies x=\frac{72}{6} \implies x=12\)

Answer: \(EF=12\)



Example 2

Given that \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) is similar to \(\bigtriangleup DEF\), where \(AB=7\), \(AC=9\), and \(DF=15\).

Find the length of \(\overline{DE}\).

Solution:

Step 1 - Identify corresponding sides

AB corresponds to DE

AC corresponds to DF

Step 2 - Set up Proportion using corresponding sides

\(\frac{AB}{DE}=\frac{AC}{DF} \implies \frac{7}{x}=\frac{9}{15}\)

Note that we used a variable x to represent the length of DE since that is what we are looking for.

Step 3 - Solve the proportion using cross-multiplication

\(\frac{7}{x}=\frac{9}{15} \implies 9\cdot x=15\cdot 7 \implies 9x=105 \implies x=\frac{105}{9} \implies x=\frac{35}{3}\)

If the answer comes out to a fraction, leave it as a fraction. DO NOT round off as a decimal. That is not an EXACT answer.

Answer: \(DE=\frac{35}{3}\)



Conclusion

In this lesson, you learned how to recognize similar triangles by identifying corresponding angles and sides. You also learned how to set up and solve a proportion to find a missing side length using the fact that corresponding sides in similar triangles are proportional. By practicing these steps, you’ll be able to solve many problems involving similar shapes, from math class to real-life scale models and diagrams. The key is to carefully match up the sides and keep your proportions balanced!
