📚 VOCABULARY

Corresponding Angles: Angles that are in the same relative position in two shapes that are similar or congruent.

Corresponding Sides: Sides that are in the same relative position in two shapes that are similar or congruent.

Dilation: A transformation that changes the size of a figure but not its shape, using a scale factor.

Proportionality: A relationship between two quantities where their ratio stays the same.

Proportional Relationship: A relationship where two variables increase or decrease at the same rate, maintaining a constant ratio.

Similarity: A relationship where two figures have the same shape, with corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides in proportion.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to use similar triangles to solve real-world problems. When two triangles are similar, their shapes are the same but their sizes may be different. This happens often in real life—like when comparing shadows, maps, scale drawings, or architectural models. Because similar triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides, we can set up and solve proportions to find unknown lengths, heights, or distances in real-world situations.


How to Use Similar Triangles in Real-World Problems

When two triangles are similar, their corresponding sides have the same ratio, which means we can create a proportion using those sides. To solve a real-world problem using similar triangles, follow these steps:

  1. Identify the two triangles in the problem.

  2. Match corresponding sides and label all known side lengths.

  3. Write a proportion using two pairs of corresponding sides.

  4. Solve for the unknown side using cross multiplication or equivalent fractions.

This method helps us solve practical problems where it would be hard or impossible to measure something directly—like the height of a tree or a building.



Example 1

A student stands next to a flagpole. The student is 5.5 feet tall and casts a shadow that is 2 feet long.

At the same time, the flagpole casts a shadow that is 8 feet long.

What is the height of the flagpole?

Solution:

Step 1 - Set up a proportion of corresponding sides or parts of the triangles formed in the image.

\(\Large \frac{person\; height}{flagpole\; height}=\frac{person\; shadow}{flagpole\; shadow}\)

\(\frac{5.5}{x}=\frac{2}{8}\)

Step 2 - Cross multiply and solve for x.

\(\frac{5.5}{x}=\frac{2}{8}\)

\(2\cdot x=8\cdot 5.5 \implies 2x=44 \implies x=\frac{44}{2} \implies x=22\)

Answer: The flagpole is 22 feet tall.



Example 2

A tree casts a 15-foot shadow at the same time that a 3-foot-tall mailbox casts a 2.5-foot shadow.

How tall is the tree?

Solution:

Step 1 - Set up a proportion of corresponding sides or parts of the triangles formed in the image.

\(\Large \frac{mailbox\; height}{tree\; height}=\frac{mailbox\; shadow}{tree\; shadow}\)

\(\frac{3}{x}=\frac{2.5}{15}\)

Step 2 - Cross multiply and solve for x.

\(\frac{3}{x}=\frac{2.5}{15}\)

\(2.5\cdot x=15\cdot 3 \implies 2.5x=45 \implies x=\frac{45}{2.5} \implies x=18\)

Answer: The tree is 18 feet tall.



Conclusion

In this lesson, you learned how to solve real-world problems by using similar triangles and proportions. By identifying corresponding sides and writing a proportion, you can solve for unknown lengths—like the height of a tree, a flagpole, or a building—without directly measuring them. This method is a powerful way to use math in everyday life. Just remember: similar triangles have matching angles and sides in proportion, and that’s the key to solving problems with them!
