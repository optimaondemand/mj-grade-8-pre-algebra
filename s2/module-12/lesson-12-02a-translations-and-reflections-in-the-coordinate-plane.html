📋 VOCABBULARY

Center of Rotation: The fixed point around which a figure turns during a rotation.

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by the intersection of a horizontal number line (x-axis) and a vertical number line (y-axis).

Dilation: A transformation that changes the size of a figure but not its shape, either enlarging or reducing it based on a scale factor.

Image: The resulting figure after a transformation is applied to the original figure.

Origin: The point where the x-axis and y-axis intersect on the coordinate plane, located at (0,0).

Preimage: The original figure before any transformation has been applied.

Reflection: A transformation that "flips" a figure over a line, creating a mirror image.

Rigid Transformation: A transformation that changes a figure’s position but not its size or shape (translations, rotations, and reflections are rigid transformations).

Rotation: A transformation that turns a figure around a fixed point, called the center of rotation.

Scale Factor: A number that describes how much a figure is enlarged or reduced during a dilation.

Translation: A transformation that slides a figure from one location to another without turning or flipping it.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to identify and describe translations and reflections when given a preimage and image on the coordinate plane. You will learn to recognize how a figure moves or flips without needing to use any algebraic rules. Instead, you will focus on describing what you see happening to the figure’s points—such as sliding up, down, left, right, or flipping over a line like the x-axis or y-axis. Understanding these transformations helps build a strong foundation for analyzing and describing movements in geometry.


Identifying Translations and Reflections

When we look at a figure and its transformed image on a coordinate plane, we first compare the locations of corresponding points.


Translations move the entire figure the same distance and direction. Every point shifts up, down, left, or right the same way.

Translations Left or Right

  • If a figure is translated \(a\) units to the right, then add \(a\) to each x-coordinate.
    • Think \((x,y)\) becomes \((x-a,y)\)
  • If a figure is translated \(a\) units to the left, then subtract \(a\) from each x-coordinate.
    • Think \((x,y)\) becomes \((x+a,y)\)

Translations Up or Down

  • If a figure is translated \(a\) units up, add \(a\) to each y-coordinate.
    • Think \((x,y)\) becomes \((x,y+a)\)
  • If a figure is translated \(a\) units down, subtract \(a\) from each y-coordinate.
    • Think \((x,y)\) becomes \((x,y-a)\)

Example:

Triangle ABC is translated left 3 units to create image A'B'C' as shown.

Note the change in the coordinates, 3 was subtracted from each x-coordinate.

\(A(3,1)\) becomes \(A'(3-3,1)=(0,1)\)

\(B(4,5)\) becomes \(B'(4-3,5)=(1,5)\)

\(C(9,4)\) becomes \(C'(9-3,4)=(6,4)\)

Example:

Triangle ABC is translated up 5 units to create image A'B'C' as shown.

Note the change in the coordinates, 5 was added to each y-coordinate.

\(A(-2,-1)\) becomes \(A'(-2,-1+5)=(-2,4)\)

\(B(1,3)\) becomes \(B'(1,3+5)=(1,8)\)

\(C(8,1)\) becomes \(C'(8,1+5)=(8,6)\)



Reflections flip the figure across a line, such as the x-axis, y-axis, or the line y = x. Each point has a matching point on the opposite side of the line at the same distance.

Reflection over the x-axis

  • The sign of the x-coordinate remains the same.
  • The sign of the y-coordinate changes to the opposite.
  • Think \((x,y)\) becomes \((x,-y)\).

Reflection over the y-axis

  • The sign of the x-coordinate changes to the opposite.
  • The sign of the y-coordinate remains the same.
  • Think \((x,y)\) becomes \((-x,y)\).

Reflection over Lines Parallel to x-axis or y-axis

  • Think about how many units each point is from the line of reflection and repeat that on the other side.
  • The line of reflection acts a mirror.

Example:

Note the coordinates from ABC to A'B'C'

\(A(2,2)\) becomes \(A'(2,-2)\)

\(B(5,6)\) becomes \(B'(5,-6)\)

\(C(10,1)\) becomes \(C'(10,-1)\)

Example:

Note the coordinates from ABC to A'B'C'

\(A(2,2)\) becomes \(A'(-2,2)\)

\(B(5,6)\) becomes \(B'(-5,6)\)

\(C(10,1)\) becomes \(C'(-10,1)\)

Example:

Note the coordinates from ABC to A'B'C'

\(A(4,1)\) becomes \(A'(1,4)\)

\(B(7,5)\) becomes \(B'(5,7)\)

\(C(12,0)\) becomes \(C'(0,12)\)



Example 1

Triangle ABC and its transformed image A'B'C' are shown on the graph.

What transformation occurred?

A) Reflection over the x-axis
B) Translation 6 units down
C) Translation 6 units right
D) Reflection over the y-axis

Solution:

  • Pay attention to what happened to the coordinates.
    • \(A(2,3)\) became \(A'(2,-3)\)
    • \(B(4,3)\) became \(B'(4,-3)\)
    • \(C(3,5)\) became \(C'(3,-5)\)
  • A reflection over the x-axis changes the y-coordinate to the opposite while leaving the x-coordinate the same.
  • We notice that this is what happens.
  • Also, in looking at the graph, the x-axis is acting as a mirror for the images.

Answer: Option A



Example 2

Quadrilateral ABCD and its transformed image A'B'C'D' are shown on the graph.

What transformation occurred?

A) Translation 4 units down
B) Reflection over the y-axis
C) Translation 4 units up
D) Reflection over the x-axis

Solution:

  • Pay attention to what happened to the coordinates.
    • \(A(-1,2)\) became \(A'(-1,6)\)
    • \(B(2,2)\) became \(B'(2,6)\)
    • \(C(2,5)\) became \(C'(2,9)\)
    • \(D(-1,5)\) became \(D'(-1,9)\)
  • All the y-coordinates increased by 4 units.
  • This tells us that the figure shifted up 4 units.
  • Also, in looking at the graph, the rectangle moved up 4 units.

Answer: Option C



Drawing a Translation or Reflection

Once you can identify transformations, you will also learn how to draw them.

  • To draw a translation, move every point the given number of spaces up, down, left, or right.

  • To draw a reflection, flip every point across the given line. Use the line as a mirror, making sure each point is the same distance from the mirror line on the other side.


Example 3

Triangle ABC shown is translated right 5 units.

Draw the image A'B'C'.

Solution:

To draw the translated image, we will want to move each point to the right 5 units.



Example 4

Quadrilateral ABCD shown is reflected over the horizontal line \(y=-3\).

Draw the image A'B'C'D'.

Solution:

To draw the reflected image, we need to mirror them over the black line representing \(y=-3\).

Think about how far each point is from that line, then go that far on the other side of the line to plot its mirror image.

Once the points are plotted, we can connect them with the polygon.



Conclusion

Today, you learned how to recognize and describe translations and reflections of figures on the coordinate plane. You practiced identifying the transformations by observing how points moved or flipped and applied those transformations by drawing new images. Remember, translations slide figures without turning or flipping, and reflections flip figures over a line like a mirror. By carefully watching how the coordinates change, you can easily describe and create these transformations without needing any complicated algebra!
